Charging curve

How the model works

  • Constant cruising speed on flat road. No wind, no temperature effects (the test data is mild weather, around 20 °C).
  • Consumption follows a curve fitted to published Model Y tests: a fixed part, an aerodynamic part that grows with speed squared, and a small standby part.
  • Charging uses an approximate Supercharger curve for a preheated battery. At 250 kW it takes roughly 30 minutes from 10 to 80%. It is scaled to the battery size and limited by the charger power.
  • Every stop adds a fixed extra time. The last stop only adds what you need to reach the destination with your chosen reserve.
  • Results are only as good as those inputs. Treat differences of a few minutes as noise.

Data used

  • Efficiency: Bjørn Nyland range tests of the Model Y (about 145 Wh/km at 90 km/h and 195–205 Wh/km at 120 km/h), reported by InsideEVs and Zerofy; 100 and 130 km/h from Techzle; 110 km/h estimate from EVKX.
  • A 2025 Model Y test at 110, 120 and 130 km/h by ENH Auto shows clearly lower consumption than the older tests. It is shown as separate dots. Use the consumption slider to move the model toward it (about 78%).
  • Charging: shape based on EVKX charging curves and the Ever Cars 10–80% test (about 31 minutes). The curve here is a simplified approximation.
